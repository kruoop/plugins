## Privacy Redaction

1. Scan the entire video for names, faces, emails, phone numbers, IDs, URLs, tokens, account/card numbers, private repositories/files, passwords, OTPs, and sensitive claims. Treat phone notification panels, inboxes, calendars, and task lists as private surfaces even when no single identifier is immediately legible.
2. Inspect multiple frames within every suspect interval. A URL can move vertically when a page scrolls or changes from a response body to a browser address bar; a handheld phone can move substantially between adjacent frames, so track the full display across the interval rather than blurring one static sample.
3. Define redactions by both time interval and pixel rectangle. A single narrow rectangle is not sufficient when the sensitive element changes position.
4. Render a draft and inspect full-resolution frames at the beginning, middle, and end of each redaction interval.
5. Reject the render if any identifier remains readable. Expand or split the redaction region and rerender.
6. Keep redaction away from the host’s face where possible; disclose unavoidable visual impact in the review notes.

## Render Workflow

1. Work from a local copy; do not mutate the source asset. Record the input's Drive ID, modified time, size and available checksum before downloading, then confirm the local bytes match. Recheck that same remote ID before delivery: editors can replace the bytes without changing the filename or ID.
2. If the source changed, compare duration and decoded audio across the full clip before reusing captions or cut timestamps. A shorter recut requires fresh alignment; equal duration alone does not prove identical cuts. If decoded audio and timing match but framing changed, retain verified timing and redo visual/privacy QA. Do not clamp or proportionally stretch old subtitles merely to fit a new edit.
3. Build transparent overlay assets and a reproducible filter graph. Before encoding, map baked-in model labels, demonstration captions, chart values and footnotes as exclusion regions alongside the presenter and spoken subtitles. Reusing the previous episode's coordinates can cover evidence even when the face remains clear; adjust only the new overlay's layout, never mask or crop the underlying evidence to make it fit. See `references/reference-led-overlay-revisions.md` for the preflight sequence.
4. Apply visual overlays only during the measured reference interval. End the opener before evidence tables appear unless the entire overlay fits verified empty space; preserve the source's test limitations and avoid converting a one-run comparison into a general superiority claim.
5. Preserve audio sync and use an upload-compatible pixel format such as `yuv420p` unless the target requires otherwise.
6. Lock output duration explicitly to the verified source duration. With looped still images, use filter-level `overlay=...:shortest=1` to end picture at source-video EOF and an explicit output `-t` for the measured source container duration. Do not use output `-frames:v` as the muxing stop when preserving copied audio: it can truncate the last AAC packet even when the video frame count is correct. Assert the frame count after encoding instead, and verify the complete copied audio hash. See the reference recipe for the command and checks.
7. Enable fast-start metadata for review/upload MP4s when appropriate.
8. Decode-test the complete output, not just probe its headers.

See `references/ffmpeg-overlay-and-redaction.md` for a tested filter-graph pattern and verification commands.

## Verification

Before delivery, verify with tools:

- exact width and height;
- frame rate;
- video and audio codecs;
- duration against the intended source;
- nonzero file size and checksum;
- complete decode with no reported errors;
- opening overlay legibility and timing;
- representative middle cuts and final frame;
- no black tail, frozen extension, clipping, broken glyphs, face obstruction, or readable private data.

A successful encode command is not proof of a correct render. Keep technical decode, sampled visual/privacy review, caption alignment, and full perceptual audio review as separate QA fields. Loudness/true-peak measurements and ASR do not establish that speech is intelligible, edits sound natural, or lip-sync is correct. Complete required listening and motion review before calling the Short finished or ready to post. If a check cannot be performed, lead the handoff with “rendered review draft; listening/motion review pending,” not “finished” followed by a caveat; Drive delivery and checksum verification do not close that gap. Carry unresolved QA fields forward through logo-only revisions: identical copied AAC preserves the old audio state, including checks never performed. At publishing handoff, distinguish the owner's explicit release approval from technical QA completion; never turn approval or a successful post into a claim that the missing review passed.

## Pitfalls

- Sampling only one frame of a moving/scrolling sensitive element.
- Switching a fixed portrait crop at the start of an animated UI transition: the preview may travel between the old and new positions for several frames. Inspect the source transition frame-by-frame and use a brief wider source view or tracked crop until the preview settles; preserve the continuous synchronized audio rather than freezing unrelated imagery.
- Trusting `-shortest` with looped PNG/SVG inputs to preserve source duration.
- Source H.264 color-parameter changes can make FFmpeg reinitialize the filter graph mid-clip, reset frame counters, drop buffered frames and restart overlays. If decode logs show filter reconfiguration while dimensions stay fixed, use input `-reinit_filter 0`, timestamp-based overlay enable, explicit verified CFR/frame count, and compare the untouched interval with frame-aligned PSNR. Apply the same reinitialization guard to contact sheets and comparison filters; header duration and audio equality alone do not prove picture timing.
- Measuring clipping only after an analysis stereo-to-mono downmix: equal-power summing can add 3 dB for correlated channels. Check native-rate, native-channel floating-point samples and the encoded true peak before changing a valid mix; retain strict overload assertions on the correct representation.
- Guessing a reference's music from a nearby asset filename. Trace its renderer first; if music is baked into the reference, verify a candidate source with distributed, sample-aligned waveform matches at one consistent offset. Mix from the independent source asset, not the reference's speech/music master, and do not interpret repetitive ASR over instrumental music as proof of vocals or clean separation.
- Letting a fallback emoji font render a blank or silhouette glyph.
- Rendering Thai with Pillow or another non-shaping path, then checking only the source string instead of the rasterized combining marks.
- Verifying a transparent overlay but not the encoded video frame; scaling or compositing can still clip punctuation, emoji, or Thai marks.
- Recutting or adding logos/redaction/design changes when the user asked to preserve a pre-cut clip and review only caption + emoji.
- Applying a template as vague inspiration instead of preserving measured placement.
- Treating a reference screenshot for one element (for example logo size/position) as permission to redesign unrelated caption typography.
- Centering two logos independently instead of centering their measured combined group.
- Changing font family, weight, size, outline, or width scaling to solve Thai shaping or line-fit problems without explicit authorization.
- Rebuilding from an older source during a narrow revision and thereby losing the most recently accepted visual state.
- Requiring decoded pixel hashes to match after a lossy inter-frame re-encode: an opening-copy change can slightly affect later reconstructed frames through encoder decisions. Verify the renderer diff preserves filters/timing, compare unchanged intervals with PSNR and frame inspection, and reserve byte-identical claims for streams actually copied (such as AAC).
- Reporting review delivery from a successful local save or Drive upload.
- Sending to a different configured messaging target after the intended recipient returns `Chat not found`.
